If running feels miserable, you're not alone. The good news: a steep, brisk walk on the treadmill gets your heart rate up nearly as much, with far less pounding on your knees.

## The setting

Our default [incline walk](/workouts/incline-walk) is:

- **15 minutes**
- **5 km/h**
- **8% incline**

That's it. Walk tall, swing your arms, and breathe.

## Why it works

- **Low impact.** Walking never has both feet off the ground, so your joints take much less stress than when jogging.
- **Harder than it looks.** The incline makes your glutes, hamstrings, and calves do real work.
- **Easy to repeat.** You'll actually want to do it again tomorrow, which matters more than any single session.

## The one mistake to avoid

Don't hang off the handrails. It feels easier because it *is* easier: you're taking your own bodyweight off the belt. If you need to hold on, lower the incline or the speed instead.

## Build up gradually

- **Week 1:** 4% incline, 4.5 km/h, 10 minutes
- **Week 2:** 6% incline, 5 km/h, 12 minutes
- **Week 3 onward:** 8% incline, 5 km/h, 15 minutes

Not ready for hills yet? Start with a flat [treadmill walk](/workouts/treadmill-walk) at 4.5 km/h. You should be able to talk in full sentences at that pace.

## When to do it

After your lifting session, or on a rest day. See the [cardio options](/workouts#cardio) for bike and elliptical alternatives.
