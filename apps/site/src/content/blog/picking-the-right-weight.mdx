Most beginners either pick a weight that's far too light (and waste the set) or far too heavy (and wreck their form). There's a simple rule that fixes both.

## The rule

Every lift in the [3-day plan](/workouts) is **3 sets of 15**. Pick a weight where the **last few reps are hard, but still clean**.

- If 15 reps feel easy, go up one step next time.
- If you can't get 12 clean reps, drop one step.
- Somewhere in between? You're in the right place. Stay there until 15 gets easy.

Controlled movement beats heavy weight, every time.

## Finding your starting weight

On a pin-loaded machine like the [leg press](/machines/leg-press) or [chest press](/machines/chest-press):

1. Set the pin somewhere light, around a quarter of the stack.
2. Do 5 slow warm-up reps. Too easy? Move the pin down one plate.
3. Repeat until the 5 reps feel like real work, but not a struggle.
4. That's your first working weight. Do your 3 × 15 from there.

With dumbbells (for example [side raises](/workouts/side-raise)), start lighter than you think. Shoulders get tired fast, and 8 kg done well beats 14 kg done by swinging.

## What "clean" means

A rep counts if you can:

- control the weight on the way down (about 2 seconds),
- avoid bouncing or throwing the weight,
- keep the joints in the positions shown on each exercise page.

If form breaks on rep 11, the set was over at rep 10, even if you finished.

## Write it down

The weight you used last time is the most useful number in the gym. Log it after every exercise and aim to go up a step every week or two. The Workoutland app tracks this for you.
